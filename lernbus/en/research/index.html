Research, clearly explained

What helps children learn?

Playing, talking, exploring — with an adult who pays attention. Research offers useful guidance on how early learning can work.

Here we explain selected findings and their limits. They concern related approaches; Lernbus itself has not yet been evaluated for effectiveness.

Sources reviewed: 25 September 2026

A closer look

Findings that inform our approach.

Who took part? What was compared? What changed? Those questions matter more than a single impressive number. The implications for Lernbus below are our interpretation.

Play & maths

Exploring, with thoughtful guidance.

A review examined 39 studies of children aged about 1–8. In guided play, children explore while adults offer prompts and work towards a learning goal.

The finding. Early maths and shape knowledge benefited compared with direct instruction. There was no general advantage for literacy or social and emotional development. Many findings relied on few studies.

For Lernbus. This supports combining play with purposeful questions and tasks. It does not establish benefits across every area of development.

  • Skene et al. (2022)Can guidance during play enhance children’s learning and development in educational contexts?

Books & language

Turning stories into conversations.

Dowdall and colleagues combined 19 randomised trials with children aged 1–6. Parents learnt to share and discuss picture books with their child.

The finding. Compared with control groups, children made greater gains in speaking and language comprehension on average. A separate review by Noble found smaller effects, with no reliable advantage over other support activities or at later assessments.

For Lernbus. Stories offer opportunities for conversation. How adults involve children matters too. These parent programmes do not directly test our lessons.

  • Dowdall et al. (2020)Shared Picture Book Reading Interventions for Child Language Development
  • Noble et al. (2019)The impact of shared book reading on children’s language skills

Relationships & learning

Supportive relationships matter.

A review brought together 26 meta-analyses of teacher–student relationships from preschool through the end of school.

The finding. Positive relationships were associated with stronger achievement, motivation and wellbeing. These associations do not show that relationships alone caused those differences.

For Lernbus. We value consistent support alongside suitable learning activities. The review establishes neither an advantage for individual lessons nor a required length of time to build trust.

  • Emslander et al. (2025)Teacher-student relationships and student outcomes

Targeted learning support

Tutoring can help learning progress.

A large review combined randomised trials of individual and small-group tutoring from preschool through the end of school.

The finding. Learning improved on average. Effects tended to be larger with younger children, trained teachers or assistants, and programmes held at least three days a week during school.

For Lernbus. This supports well-guided, targeted help. Programmes differed in many ways, so these comparisons establish neither a minimum frequency nor an expected effect for our weekly hour.

  • Nickow, Oreopoulos & Quan (2024)The Promise of Tutoring for PreK–12 Learning

Time & frequency

Short sessions can help too.

BEST was compared with another language programme in 102 monolingual English-speaking children aged about 3½–4½ with weak language skills. Speech and language therapists delivered small-group sessions twice a week for eight weeks, lasting 15 minutes each.

The finding. BEST produced greater expressive-language gains. There was no clear difference in comprehension between programmes immediately afterwards or at follow-up.

For Lernbus. A focused learning goal can benefit from limited weekly time. This was specialist English-language support, not general early learning or German-language provision.

  • McKean et al. (2025)A Cluster Randomized Controlled Trial Comparing the Efficacy of Pre-School Language Interventions

Language in everyday learning

Reading, talking and practising regularly.

The independent 2026 NELI Preschool trial included 3,298 children aged 3–4 in 303 English settings. Settings were randomly assigned. Over 20 weeks, daily 15–20-minute language activities were supplemented, for children with weaker language, by three 10–15-minute small-group sessions and one 5–10-minute individual session each week.

The finding. Oral language improved by a small, statistically reliable amount compared with usual provision. Long-term findings are still pending.

For Lernbus. This supports structured language activities. The whole package was tested, including digital materials. It establishes neither the added benefit of individual sessions nor the effect of our screen-free format.

  • Roy et al. / NFER (2026)Effectiveness Trial of Nuffield Early Language Intervention – Preschool
  • Education Endowment Foundation (2026)NELI Preschool: evaluation and reports

Group size

Smaller is not automatically better.

ROOTS randomly assigned US kindergarten children with weak maths skills to groups of two, groups of five or usual teaching. Support comprised 50 additional sessions: 20 minutes each, five days a week.

The finding. The intervention improved three of six measured outcomes significantly. No achievement advantage was detected for pairs over groups of five.

For Lernbus. Small groups can be useful. This study proves neither that all group sizes are equivalent nor that children should spend a semester learning individually before moving to pairs.

  • Clarke et al. (2017) · PDFTesting the Efficacy of a Kindergarten Mathematics Intervention by Small Group Size

Swiss research

The specific programme matters.

The University of Basel studied German-language support before kindergarten in 2022/23 and 2023/24, then offered for five hours a week. A separate Swiss project, KOMPAS 2, tested additional SPRINT support in kindergarten.

The finding. The Basel analysis found benefits especially for children with low starting skills. It used the assignment threshold rather than randomisation; direct testing involved a volunteer subsample. In the randomised KOMPAS 2 comparison, SPRINT brought no demonstrated additional benefit over usual German-language support.

For Lernbus. Local research is valuable. It also shows that adding support does not automatically add benefit. Both programmes differ substantially from Lernbus.

  • Universität Basel / Marina Jambreus (2025) · PDFPrüfung der Wirksamkeit früher Deutschförderung, Kanton Basel-Stadt
  • Sammann et al. / HfH (2026)KOMPAS – Kommunikative Partizipation und Sprachförderung bei Kindern mit DaZ

Our interpretation

What does this mean for Lernbus?

Is one hour a week enough?

There is no universal minimum. BEST used brief, focused sessions; NELI and ROOTS involved daily activities. Goals, methods and children’s needs differ. These findings neither establish that Lernbus’s weekly hour is effective nor show that it is ineffective.

Why individually, then in pairs?

Lernbus starts with one semester of individual support and then works in pairs. This is our educational design. The research summarised here does not establish that this sequence or a semester of individual lessons is necessary. The group-size study offers no general rule that smaller is better.

What do we hope children will gain?

We combine personal support with playful work on language and early maths. Confidence, curiosity, persistence and independent thinking are also our goals. They are not yet demonstrated outcomes of Lernbus, and findings about specific skills do not establish lifelong benefits.

This is a curated selection, not a complete systematic review. We prioritise original studies and research reviews relevant to our age group and methods, including findings without an additional benefit. Most studies took place in other programmes and settings.
