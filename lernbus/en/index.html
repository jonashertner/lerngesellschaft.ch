Strengthening today's children for tomorrow

Learning at their own pace, with a person at their side.

The Learning Bus is a preventive, pedagogically grounded support programme for children in their early learning years. It is open to every child, whatever they bring with them and wherever they come from. We begin early, while confidence and the joy of learning are still intact.

At the centre is the lasting development of a positive relationship to learning. In this way the Learning Bus strengthens children to shape their own educational path with confidence, independence, and an eye on what is ahead.

Why the Learning Bus is needed

Our world is changing fast, and it already asks new things of children. Alongside subject knowledge, what will count most is independence, the ability to solve problems, creativity, and a constructive way of meeting difficulty. National assessments of basic competences show that not all children reach the expected minimum standards in language and mathematics even in primary school: according to Switzerland's national assessment, already in the second year of primary school almost one child in four does not reach basic competence in mathematics, and in reading it is just over one in five.

Beginning early rather than correcting late

Children with a greater need for support, or with fewer resources for learning at home, are affected most. Without early support, gaps risk hardening into lasting disadvantage. At the same time all children benefit from the programme, because the capacities that will matter are deliberately built. The Learning Bus begins early and strengthens children as a whole, before difficult experiences of learning set in.

Creating the conditions, not forcing the result

Learning cannot be ordered into being. As with a plant, you cannot make the growing happen; you can only create the conditions for it: time, attention, something worth wondering at, and a person who watches and believes the child can. That is precisely the work of the Learning Bus.

Approach and effect

What we work on with the children.

The relationship comes first

Children learn from people, not from programmes. What a child dares to ask depends on how safe it feels. That is why the same person stays at its side for months: someone who knows its name, knows its detours, and remembers what it was working on last week. This reliability is not a pleasant extra. It is the ground everything else stands on.

Starting broad, not narrowing early

At this age the point is not to find one talent and drop everything else. Children need breadth first: building, counting, telling, drawing, examining something outdoors. A child who has tried many things finds it easier later to find what really fits, and can carry what it has learnt from one field into another. An early lead in a single subject says little about how far a child will go. And no child is without talent: talents simply look different from one another, and school cannot make all of them equally visible.

Future capacities and foundations at once

The Learning Bus is oriented towards central future capacities: emotional intelligence, creativity, communication, and critical, connected thinking. Children learn to think for themselves, to see how things connect, to develop their own ways to a solution, and to use mistakes as chances to learn. They are given strategies for opening up even the less exciting material, and for staying with something when the first impulse is reluctance. Alongside this, gaps in the basic competences of language and mathematics are deliberately worked through and consolidated.

Learning needs visible progress

Children develop a love of learning above all when they experience that their own effort leads to success. Progress they notice themselves strengthens their confidence in their own abilities, increases their persistence, and makes them readier to take on something new. Research from Erasmus University Rotterdam indicates that people learn with the greatest concentration and persistence when they can perceive their own progress. The Learning Bus makes learning visible and creates the moments of success that sustain motivation and a positive relationship to learning.

The right challenge at the right time

Learning works best when a task matches a child's individual stage of development. The support therefore follows the child's interests, strengths and starting point. It happens in the optimal learning zone: demanding enough to prompt development, and shaped so that success remains possible. Mistakes are understood as an important part of learning and used as opportunities to learn. A child who is not allowed to get anything wrong eventually stops trying anything new.

Early German language support

Language is the foundation of learning, of belonging, and of success in education. Early language support helps children from the age of three to build the foundations of language, to express themselves with more assurance, and to take part in lessons with more confidence. It too takes place with one or two children at a time. The programme strengthens children in their confidence, their resilience, and their readiness to learn independently.

A protected space, without screens

The learning space is trusting, protected, and built on what the child already brings. Screens have no place in it: a screen interrupts this space and replaces the slow way to an answer with a quick one. The work is done with books, paper and pencils, with a magnifying glass, scales and clay, and with whatever can be found outdoors. The long way round is not lost time: what a child has worked out with effort stays, while what went down quickly and smoothly is often gone again just as quickly.

Goals of the programme

What the support works towards.

  • Strengthening learning competence, self-worth and self-efficacy
  • Fostering curiosity and intrinsic motivation
  • Developing resilience and ways of dealing with setbacks
  • Building a safe, appreciative space for learning
  • Fostering communication and independent thinking
  • Making individual progress visible
  • Teaching strategies for material that does not interest them
  • Trying many things rather than settling early on one

Who it is for, and how to get in

Who the Learning Bus is there for.

  • Children from the first year of kindergarten to the third year of primary school (roughly ages four to nine)
  • Particularly suited to children whose learning and future capacities would benefit from deliberate support
  • Early German language support from age 3, with one or two children at a time
  • Subsidised and donation-funded places, so that opportunity does not depend on income
  • Access through schools, kindergartens, day-care centres and parents

How it works

From first conversation to the learning box.

1

A first conversation

We sit down with the parents: what does this child need, what do you hope for, what is possible.

2

The right mentor

The child is matched with a learning mentor who fits their interests and their needs.

3

The sessions

60 to 90 minutes with one or two children, over at least six months. The content follows the child's interests and brings together the creative, the linguistic, the mathematical and the emotional.

4

The learning box

Ideas, work, questions and discoveries collect in a personal Lernkiste that makes progress visible.

5

Regular talks with parents

We stay in touch with the parents and support child and family as needed, including accompaniment to meetings where that helps.

6

Small groups in the school holidays

In the holidays, small groups deepen and consolidate what has been worked on during the term.

From the Lernhaltestelle into the neighbourhoods

We start at the Lernhaltestelle, our learning stop at Münsterplatz: a fixed, quiet place where children arrive and to which they gladly return. Later the Learning Bus joins in and drives to where the children are, right into the neighbourhoods. The way to learning should be a short one.

Making learning visible

Over the course of the support, children document their ideas, their work, their questions and their discoveries in a personal Lernkiste, a learning box of their own. This makes individual development and learning visible, and open to reflection. The approach follows the strengths-based understanding of education in the Reggio Emilia tradition. What matters is not the result alone, but the path towards it. Children experience that their thoughts, their ideas and their progress are valuable and are noticed. Nothing is marked, everything is kept, and the box belongs to the child.

Open to all, made possible for many

Families can book the Learning Bus. Contributions follow the family's income, and subsidised and donation-funded places make sure that every child can take part, even when money at home is tight. When you fund a place, you give a child time with a person who believes in them.

A girl is working on a mathematics problem that seemed unsolvable to her a few weeks ago. She thinks, discards one approach, tries again, and finally finds her own way to the answer. In that moment more than a subject goal is reached: the child experiences herself as capable and able to act.

When self-efficacy becomes real

Experiences of self-efficacy like this one are at the heart of the Learning Bus. Children recognise that their effort has an effect, that learning is a process of development, and that difficulty can be met through persistence, curiosity and their own thinking. Out of this grows a positive and lasting relationship to learning.

Conditions and quality

Who works with the children.

Our learning mentors

Our learning mentors work reflectively, with empathy, and on a sound professional footing. Because they come from a range of professional backgrounds, children have varied and well-matched people to turn to, who can pick up on their individual interests and needs.

Quality that is checked

The quality of the programme is secured and continually developed through team meetings, professional exchange, evaluation, and further training.

Who runs it

The Learning Bus is one of Lerngesellschaft's three paths.

Lerngesellschaft is an association (Verein) based in Basel. It fosters the natural curiosity and the will to learn of children and young people along three paths: the Forscherbühne, the Learning Bus, and Brain and Society.
