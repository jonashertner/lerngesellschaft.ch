Helping children enjoy learning from the start

Learning at their own pace, with someone by their side.

Lernbus is an educational programme offering early, preventive support for children. It is open to all children, whatever their abilities or background. We start early to nurture their confidence and enjoyment of learning.

Our focus is on building a lasting, positive relationship with learning. Lernbus helps children shape their education with confidence, independence and a sense of direction for the future.

Why Lernbus is needed

Our world is changing rapidly, placing new demands on children even today. Alongside subject knowledge, independence, problem-solving, creativity and the ability to respond constructively to challenges will become increasingly important. National assessments show that some children are already falling short of the expected minimum standards in language and mathematics at primary school. In Switzerland's national assessment of basic skills, almost one in four children in the second year of primary school did not meet the basic standard in mathematics; in reading, the figure was just over one in five.

Early support, before difficulties take hold

Children who need additional support or have fewer learning resources are particularly affected. Without early support, gaps can become entrenched and lead to lasting disadvantage. The programme also benefits children more broadly by helping them develop skills for the future. Lernbus starts early and supports the whole child, before negative experiences of learning take hold.

Creating the conditions for learning

Learning cannot happen on command. Just as with a plant, we cannot force growth; we can create the conditions it needs: time, care, something that sparks wonder, and someone who pays attention and believes in the child. That is the work of Lernbus.

Our educational approach

What we help children develop.

The relationship comes first

Children learn through relationships with people, not programmes alone. The questions a child feels able to ask depend on how safe they feel. That is why the same person stays by their side over the months: someone who knows their name, understands how they learn and remembers what they were working on last week. That continuity is the foundation for everything else.

Room to explore before specialising

At this age, children need opportunities to explore, rather than being steered towards a single talent. They need variety: building, counting, telling stories, drawing and investigating things outdoors. Trying different activities helps children discover what suits them and apply what they have learnt in new contexts. An early advantage in one subject tells us little about how far a child will go. Every child has talents; they take different forms, and school does not reveal them all equally well.

Foundations for learning and skills for the future

Lernbus focuses on key skills for the future: emotional intelligence, creativity, communication, critical thinking and the ability to make connections. Children learn to think independently, recognise relationships between ideas, find solutions and use mistakes as opportunities to learn. They develop strategies for engaging with less interesting material and keeping going when they feel reluctant. Alongside this, we address gaps in language and mathematical skills and strengthen those foundations.

Progress children can see

Children develop a love of learning when they see their own efforts lead to success. Recognising their progress builds confidence in their abilities, encourages persistence and helps them feel ready for new challenges. Research from Erasmus University Rotterdam suggests that people learn with particular focus and persistence when they can see their own progress. Lernbus makes learning visible and creates experiences of success that support lasting motivation and a positive attitude towards learning.

The right challenge at the right time

Children learn best when tasks match their stage of development. We therefore build on each child's interests, strengths and existing skills. Activities offer enough challenge to encourage development while keeping success within reach. Mistakes are an important part of learning and an opportunity to understand more. A child who is never allowed to make mistakes eventually stops trying new things.

Early German language support

Language underpins learning, participation and success in education. Early German language support helps children from the age of three build language skills, express themselves more confidently and feel more assured in class. They learn through play, stories, songs and conversations, because young children learn most through play. Lessons take place one to one or in pairs. The programme builds children's confidence, resilience and readiness to learn independently.

A safe learning space, without screens

We create a safe, trusting learning environment that builds on each child's strengths. Screens have no place here: they interrupt this space and replace the gradual process of finding an answer with a quick response. Instead, we use books, paper and pencils, magnifying glasses, scales and clay, and things we find outdoors. Taking the longer route is not wasted time. What children work out through their own effort stays with them; what comes quickly and easily can disappear just as quickly.

Goals of the programme

What we aim to achieve.

  • Strengthening learning skills, self-worth and self-efficacy
  • Encouraging curiosity and intrinsic motivation
  • Developing resilience and ways of dealing with setbacks
  • Building a safe learning space where children feel valued
  • Developing communication skills and independent thinking
  • Making individual progress visible
  • Teaching strategies for engaging with less interesting material
  • Trying many things rather than settling early on one

Who can take part

Who Lernbus is for.

  • Children from age 3 to the third year of primary school
  • Particularly suited to children who would benefit from focused support with learning skills and skills for the future
  • Early German language support from age 3, with one or two children at a time
  • Enquiries from schools, kindergartens, childcare centres and parents

How it works

From the first meeting to the learning box.

1

An introductory meeting

We meet with you and your child to discuss what your child needs, what you hope for and how we can help.

2

The right mentor

We match your child with a mentor suited to their interests and needs.

3

The sessions

Each lesson lasts 60 minutes and takes place one to one or in pairs. Lessons are booked for a school semester. Activities follow the child's interests, combining play with creative, language, mathematical and emotional development.

4

The learning box

Each child collects their ideas, work, questions and discoveries in a personal learning box, or Lernkiste, making their progress visible.

5

Regular meetings with parents

We stay in touch with parents and offer support to both child and family as needed, including attending meetings with them where helpful. Our conversations draw on the child's documented development and learning box, which show their progress over the months.

6

Small groups in the school holidays

During the holidays, children meet in small groups to build on and consolidate what they have learnt during the school semester.

From Münsterplatz into the neighbourhoods

We begin at Münsterplatz: a quiet base where children can settle in and look forward to returning. Later, the Lernbus vehicle will bring the programme into local neighbourhoods, closer to where children live. Learning should be within easy reach.

Making learning visible

Children document their ideas, work, questions and discoveries in their own learning box, or Lernkiste. It makes their learning and development visible and gives them a way to reflect on it. This approach draws on the strengths-based educational philosophy of Reggio Emilia. The process matters as much as the result. Children see that their thoughts, ideas and progress are valued and noticed. Nothing is graded, everything is documented, and the box belongs to the child.

Open to all, made possible for many

We offer four rates for each 60-minute lesson: subsidised, reduced, full-cost and solidarity. For lessons in pairs, the fee is per child. Some places are funded by people who invest in children, helping families take part when money is tight. Funding a place gives a child time with someone who believes in them.

When self-efficacy becomes real

Experiences like this are at the heart of Lernbus. Children discover that their efforts make a difference, that learning develops over time and that they can meet challenges through persistence, curiosity and independent thought. This builds a lasting, positive relationship with learning.

Who runs it

Lernbus is a programme of Lerngesellschaft.

Lerngesellschaft (Learning Society) is an association based in Basel. It encourages children's and young people's natural curiosity and desire to learn through three programmes: Forscherbühne, Lernbus, and Brain and Society.
