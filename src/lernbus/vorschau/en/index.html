For parents in Basel

Individual learning support that makes children strong.

The Learning Bus is a preventive, pedagogically grounded programme for children in their early learning years. Our world is changing fast and already places new demands on children. Beyond subject knowledge, what will matter most is independence, problem-solving, creativity and a constructive way of handling challenges.

Every child benefits from the Learning Bus, because it deliberately builds the competencies that matter for the future. It starts early and strengthens children as a whole, before negative learning experiences can take hold. At the centre stands the lasting development of a positive relationship with learning.

Pedagogical approach and impact

The Learning Bus is guided by core competencies such as emotional intelligence, creativity, the ability to communicate, and critical, connected thinking.

Children learn to think for themselves, to recognise connections, to develop their own paths to a solution and to use mistakes as chances to learn.

They gain strategies for actively engaging with material that is not immediately exciting, and for staying with it through initial reluctance.

In parallel, gaps in basic language and mathematical skills can be addressed and consolidated in a targeted way. Early German support helps children from the age of three to build language foundations, express themselves more confidently and take part in lessons with greater trust.

The programme strengthens children's self-confidence, their resilience and their readiness to learn independently.

Is this for my child?

Yes. The Learning Bus is for every child.

Your child does not need to be struggling to take part. Most often, parents come to us in one of these situations:

  • Your child has lost the pleasure of learning and no longer wants to talk about school.
  • They are falling behind in class, in reading, in maths, or in both.
  • They are not stretched enough and are bored.
  • They are learning German and need someone alongside them while they do.
  • They expect little of themselves, although they can do a great deal.
  • Everyday life leaves little room for practising regularly.

And if none of this applies, your child is just as welcome.

Early German language support

When German is still new.

Some children grow up with another language and arrive at kindergarten with little German. That is not a delay, it is a beginning. From the age of three we help children build German up: understanding, speaking, telling a story, and word by word a vocabulary that carries.

It happens playfully, in stories, songs, pictures and conversations about whatever is on your child's mind. Alone or with one other child, with the same person, who takes the time and listens closely. Your child's first language stays welcome throughout.

What a meeting looks like

Not a lesson. No marks.

It begins with a question

The mentor meets your child where it actually stands, and builds on what interests it. The work is done with books, paper and pencils, with a magnifying glass, scales and clay, and with whatever can be found outdoors. Screens have no place in it. And it stays deliberately broad: building, drawing, counting, telling. A child who tries many things finds out more easily what suits them.

There is practice, but no testing

Gaps in reading, writing and arithmetic are worked through calmly and deliberately. Alongside that, it is always about what lasts longer: thinking independently, staying with something, asking questions, being allowed to get things wrong.

Something remains

Ideas, work, questions and discoveries collect in a personal Lernkiste, a learning box. Nothing is marked, everything is kept, and the box belongs to your child. Over the months you can see in it what has grown.

A girl works at a task that seemed impossible to her a few weeks earlier. She thinks, discards approaches, tries again, and finally finds a way through on her own. In that moment she is not only learning mathematics. She is experiencing herself as someone who can do something.

Common questions

What parents ask us.

Is this tutoring?

Not in the usual sense. We work on gaps where there are gaps. Above all, though, it is about your child learning gladly and independently. That lasts longer than any single topic.

Does my child need to have problems?

No. The Learning Bus is open to every child, whatever they bring with them and wherever they come from. We deliberately begin early, while confidence and the joy of learning are still intact.

Is this not too early?

Early does not mean more pressure, and it does not mean school material brought forward. Young children learn through play and within a secure relationship, and that is exactly where we begin. The point is that learning stays joined to joy from the start.

Why at least six months?

Because trust takes time. The effect does not come from any single exercise, but from a reliable relationship with a person who knows your child and remembers what it was working on last week.

Who works with my child?

Learning mentors from a range of professional backgrounds. Quality is maintained continuously through team meetings, professional exchange, evaluation and further training.

One child or two?

Both are possible. Some children concentrate better alone, others come alive with a second child there, because explaining and listening are themselves ways of learning. What suits your child we decide together in the introductory conversation.

And during the school holidays?

In the holidays there are small groups that deepen and consolidate what was worked on during the term.
