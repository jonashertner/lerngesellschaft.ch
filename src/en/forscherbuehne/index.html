Forscherbühne

Science to wonder at and think along with. On the Forscherbühne, children and young people experience science directly and authentically: researchers perform real experiments live in front of an audience and make scientific phenomena vivid and understandable.

Free of charge and open to all

The Forscherbühne understands education as an opportunity for everyone. Participation is therefore planned to be free and open regardless of background, language or social situation. Children and young people are invited to come with their families, school classes or carers. The Forscherbühne draws on a long tradition of public science communication, as practised by the Royal Institution in London since 1825 with its Christmas Lectures for young people, and brings this spirit to Basel.

Shared wonder as the starting point

When an experiment produces an unexpected reaction, when something lights up, moves or visibly changes, wonder arises. Out of wonder come questions, and questions are the starting point for understanding, discovery and independent thought. The Forscherbühne creates moments in which children want, from their own curiosity, to find out how things connect.

Researchers as models

The experiments are carried out by scientists who share their enthusiasm for research and discovery. What matters is not spectacle, but understanding scientific connections together. Children encounter researchers as people who observe carefully, ask questions and test explanations.

Learning through questions, trials and errors

Scientific knowledge rarely arrives by a direct route. Children experience that doing research means forming hypotheses, testing ideas and learning from what fails. It is precisely in that process that persistence, critical thinking and problem solving grow. Young children put their hands up even when they are not yet sure of the answer; we want to protect and nurture that fearlessness.

Science to take home

So that the joy of discovery continues beyond the event, children receive ideas for experiments of their own using simple everyday materials. In this way inquiry enters family life and encourages children to follow their questions independently.

An illustrative example A girl is sitting in the front row. At the front, two clear liquids are mixed together, and suddenly the solution glows an intense blue. For a moment everything is completely still. The girl looks up at her father in astonishment, then turns back to the experiment and asks quietly: how does that work?

This is exactly where science begins

Out of wonder comes a question, and out of the question the wish to understand the world better. Together with the research team, the children follow up on what they have observed, form hypotheses, compare explanations and uncover step by step the scientific connections behind the phenomenon. Children experience that knowledge is not merely handed down, but built through observing, asking and testing.
