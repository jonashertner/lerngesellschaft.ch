Pillar · The Learning Bus

The Learning Bus

The Learning Bus drives to where the children are, right into the neighbourhood. Inside sits one child with a mentor who is there for this one child alone. The mentor sees where the child is today, and goes on from there with them.

Starting where the child is

The Learning Bus accompanies children from around age three, even before kindergarten, up to the third year of primary school. Every child is at a different point. For school children, we therefore speak briefly with the class teacher before we begin: what is the class working on, where is this child, what would help. Then the mentor finds the task that fits right now, not too easy and not too hard. That way the child stays with it and notices, time after time, how they are getting better. With the youngest, before and in kindergarten, we pursue no school learning goals, we protect the space for play.

The same person, over a long time

With the Learning Bus, it is always the same mentor. A child comes to the same person every week, for one hour, for a whole term and often longer. Every session follows the same calm arc: arriving, picking up last week's thread, thirty to forty-five minutes of absorbed work, and at the end looking together at what has taken shape. Screens have no place in the session: a screen interrupts this space and replaces the slow way to an answer with a quick one. The work is done with books, paper and pencils, with a magnifying glass, scales and clay, and with whatever can be found outdoors. Out of the meetings a relationship grows, one in which the child dares to take a chance, to make mistakes, and to ask. Our mentors are chosen for attitude and aptitude, carefully trained, and supervised.

A book that belongs to the child

At the end of the term, a bound, printed book is made for each child, with their own words, drawings and photographs. It holds the journey, not just the result: what the child worked on, what they thought and what they asked. Nothing is marked, everything is kept. The book is meant to show the child: I thought of this myself. It belongs to the child. Parents receive a copy, and for school children so does the teacher. So the child arrives back in their class stronger.

Open to all, made possible for many

Families can book the Learning Bus. Contributions are scaled to what a family can afford, and reduced fees and free places make sure that no child is left out for lack of money. We fund the free places through investment. When you invest in a place, you give a child time with a person who believes in them.

A girl is working out a problem that was still too hard for her last week. She pauses. The mentor waits and says nothing. Then the answer comes from her, all by herself. She looks up, the mentor just nods, and she beams, because this time she managed it on her own.
