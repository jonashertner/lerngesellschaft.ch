What this is about

Curiosity is the most precious thing a child brings with them.

Every child wants, all on their own, to discover, to learn and to understand.

Our world is changing fast, and it already asks new things of children. What counts more and more is independence, the ability to solve problems, creativity, and a constructive way of meeting difficulty. At the same time, national assessments show that not all children reach the expected minimum standards in language and mathematics even in primary school: according to Switzerland's national assessment of basic competences (ÜGK), already in the second year of primary school almost one child in four does not reach basic competence in mathematics, and in reading it is just over one in five.

Meanwhile learning is increasingly replaced by passive consumption. But a society fit for the future needs curious children who can experience, test and explore their learning actively. All children benefit, because exactly those capacities are built that will matter most. This is where we begin. So that as many children as possible can take part, we rely, as an association, on people who invest alongside us.

Our goals

What we are working towards.

01

Motivation and learning competence

We deliberately strengthen children's natural curiosity and their own motivation to learn, so that they develop a lasting readiness to learn and experience lifelong learning as something they direct themselves.

02

The spirit of inquiry

Children experience research, experiment and discovery at first hand. In this way curiosity, the joy of finding out, and an inquiring disposition are sustained over the long term.

03

Social and emotional competence

Children learn to notice, name and reflect on their own feelings. This strengthens their self-awareness and an empathetic, respectful way of treating themselves and others.

04

German as the language of school

Children with German as a second language build both everyday and academic language. This strengthens their ability to communicate, their participation in education, and their equality of opportunity.

Three paths

Three paths, one aim.

The Forscherbühne, our researchers' stage, awakens wonder and makes science something children and young people in Basel can experience directly. The Learning Bus accompanies each child individually, at their own pace, reliably alongside a person who builds trust and makes development possible. With Brain and Society we bring current knowledge into school about how learning works, how social competence grows, and what part people and technologies play in education.

Our work follows the principles of the Reggio Emilia approach: children are understood as active shapers of their own education, and their paths of learning are made visible. Three paths, one aim: to support children in understanding the world, growing into their potential, and feeling understood in what makes them singular.

Three convictions run through everything we do. Children learn from people, not from programmes. They need breadth first, not an early specialism. And they have to be allowed to get things wrong, or they will never risk anything of their own.

The association

Who is behind Learning Society.

Learning Society is an association (Verein) based in Basel, the classic Swiss legal form for a community initiative. It is carried by people from education and law who are at home in Basel.

The board gives its time voluntarily.
