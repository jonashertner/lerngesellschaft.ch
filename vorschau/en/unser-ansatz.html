How we work

How we work

A child learns best when two things come together. They feel themselves moving forward. And they know there is a person who knows them and believes they can do this. Everything we do is built from these two things.

A child stays with it when they feel themselves getting better

A child stays with something when they notice they are making progress. Every small step they see in themselves gives them the courage for the next one. Progress builds up slowly, so staying with it is worth far more than a quick try. And when a child is moving forward, they forget the time and are wholly with what they are doing.

Just within reach

What a child likes best is whatever is just within reach, not too easy and not too hard. Then they know where they want to go, and practising starts to make sense. A quick, kind answer shows them what comes next. A mistake here is not a failure, it shows where the way leads on. And what a child tries out for themselves, they truly understand.

Someone who stays

Trust grows slowly, and only with people who stay. When a child feels that someone really knows them, they dare to do more. They ask a question, try something new, and are allowed to get it wrong sometimes. That is why, with us, the same child comes to the same person every week, for a whole term and often longer. That person thinks about the child between the sessions and brings back the question that was left open last week. The loveliest feeling for a child is that someone is counting on them and believes they can manage.

What a machine cannot do

The more screens there are around a child, the more the person beside them matters. A screen can praise a child without end, but it cannot believe in them. It does not notice when a child is sad or having a hard day. To find your way back to someone after a quarrel, a child can only learn that with a person who helps them do so. And a child who is only ever praised never learns to handle a disappointment, and that is exactly what a child needs in life.

Three pillars, one aim

The Forscherbühne awakens wonder and turns real science into something you can experience, open to everyone in Basel. The Learning Bus goes with each child at their own pace, alongside someone who stays. Brain and Society brings into the classroom how learning, and getting along well together, can work, and what a machine can and cannot do. Three paths, one aim: that a child learns to understand, and feels understood.

Every child wants to understand, and to be understood.
