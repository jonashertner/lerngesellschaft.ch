Pillar · Brain and Society

Brain and Society

We come into the primary school and work with whole classes. In short, simple sessions, the children come to understand how their own learning works, how to get along well together, and what a machine can do and what only a person can do.

How learning works

First we look at how learning happens in the head. The children see that getting better is completely normal, and that it feels good. A mistake is not a flaw, it is a sign of where the way leads on. Once a child has felt this, they will take on something that only yesterday seemed too hard.

How we are good together

The second part is about being together. How do we talk to one another, even when we are cross. What do we do when there is a quarrel. The children practise putting a friendship right again after a falling out, and they find that it can be done.

What a machine can and cannot do

At the end we talk about artificial intelligence, simply and honestly. What can it do well, where does it get things wrong, and why does a person stay something no machine can replace. A machine can say a great deal, but it does not notice when a child is sad, and it is not glad for you.

A boy has got a task wrong three times and wants to rub it out. An adult sits down beside him and says calmly: show me how you were thinking. They go through it together, find the one place where it tips over, and the fourth time it is right. The boy looks up and laughs, half surprised at himself, and says: so I could do it after all.
